学生“慢科研”联合倡议

——致所有有志于科研的本科生们

（本声明也供在读硕士生和博士生参考）

我们是一群来自国内外高校与科研院所的计算机科学、人工智能及相关领域的教师和硕士生及博士生导师。我们共同签署这份声明，想对你们说几句心里话。

一、我们观察到的现象

近年来，人工智能研究蓬勃发展，令人振奋。但我们也注意到一种令人担忧的趋势：越来越多的本科学生急于进入实验室，急于在短期内完成一篇论文并投往顶会；越来越多的博士申请者简历上列着数篇论文，但当被问及论文的技术细节、研究动机与失败经历时，却难以作答。

我们理解你们的焦虑——当身边的人都在发论文时，不发论文似乎就意味着落后。但请相信我们：这种以数量为目标做科研的做法，正在伤害你们最宝贵的东西——真正做科研的能力。

二、我们想明确告诉你们的

  1. 论文数量不是我们选拔博士生的核心指标。 在面试中，我们考察的是：你是否真正理解自己的工作？你是否清楚每一个设计决策背后的原因？你能否分析它的局限并设想改进方向？一篇被你吃透的论文，远比十篇你只挂了名字的论文更有说服力。
  2. 短时间内灌水式地产出多篇论文，在我们的评估中是减分项，不是加分项。 两三个月完成一篇“顶会论文”，在绝大多数情况下意味着：问题不是你提出的，方法不是你设计的，论文甚至不是你写的。面试中经不起追问的文章，只会让评审老师对你的科研理解产生疑虑。
  3. 科研的目的不是论文，而是能力的塑造。 科研训练的本质，是学会发现重要的问题、批判性地分析现有工作、在不确定中做出判断、诚实地面对失败。这些能力的养成没有捷径——需要你花时间读透经典工作，为一个结果反复验证与怀疑，忍受长期的“没有产出”。

慢慢来，才能走得远。

三、我们的承诺

作为签署这份声明的教师，我们承诺：

在博士招生中，不将论文数量作为筛选标准，重点考察学生的理解深度、思维品质与科研潜力；对短时间灌水式发表保持审慎态度，在面试中深入追问论文细节与研究过程；在各自的实验室中，我们将把“培养独立思考能力”置于“产出论文”之上。

论文是科研的副产品，思考才是科研的本体。一个真正学会思考的人，永远不会被时代的浪潮落下。

Joint Statement on Slow Research

To Undergraduates Considering Research in Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence

We are faculty members, researchers, and graduate supervisors in computer science, artificial intelligence, and related fields at universities and research institutes worldwide. We sign this statement to offer a few words to students beginning their research journeys.

I   What We Have Observed

The growth of artificial intelligence research is exciting. Yet in our work with students, we have also seen a troubling pattern: some undergraduates enter labs hoping to produce a paper for a top venue as quickly as possible. Some PhD applicants list several papers on their CVs but struggle to explain the motivation, technical decisions, or setbacks behind the work.

We understand the pressure. When everyone around you seems to be publishing, it is easy to feel left behind. But focusing only on paper counts only may cause you to miss the real value of doing research.

II   What We Want to Tell You

  1. Paper count is not our main criterion for selecting PhD students. In interviews, we will ask: Do you understand your own work? Can you explain the key design decisions? Can you discuss its limitations and possible improvements? We care more about what you contributed and how well you understand the work than about how many papers you have published.
  2. Producing more papers at the expense of quality counts against you. Completing a paper in two or three months does not, by itself, demonstrate research ability. We want to know what you contributed and what you learned. If you cannot explain the work under detailed questioning, the publication alone will not convince us that you are ready for doctoral research.
  3. Research training should develop your ability to think independently. Learning to do research means identifying important questions, examining existing work critically, making judgments under uncertainty, and acknowledging setbacks and learning from unsuccessful approaches. This takes time: reading foundational work carefully, checking results, questioning assumptions, and sometimes working for long periods without a publishable result.

Take the time to build these skills. They will support your research throughout your career.

III   Our Commitment

As signatories to this statement, we commit to the following:

In PhD admissions, we will not screen applicants simply by counting papers, but will focus on depth of understanding, quality of thinking, and research potential. In interviews, we will focus on what applicants contributed and how they conducted their research, rather than judging applicants by their publication records alone. In our own labs, we will put the development of independent thinking ahead of publication counts.

Papers are one outcome of research; careful thinking is at its heart.
